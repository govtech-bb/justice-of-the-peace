Justice of the Peace

A Justice of the Peace (JP) can help you with official documents. Find out what a JP does and how to find one near you.

Last updated: 21 May 2026

Who is a Justice of the Peace?

A Justice of the Peace (JP) is someone appointed by the President of Barbados to help people with official documents. It is a volunteer role — JPs are not paid.

JPs are ordinary people from your community. They could be teachers, nurses, farmers, accountants or church leaders. When they are chosen, their parish and job are written down.

Once someone becomes a JP, they stay a JP for life unless they choose to stop or are removed. Before they start, they make a promise to serve honestly at an official ceremony.

JPs do this job for free. They are not allowed to charge you. If you already know a JP — a neighbour, a teacher, a church elder, someone you work with — you can just go to them.

What can a JP help you with?

JP help is free. You do not pay a JP.

Check a copy of a document

A JP can look at your original document and a copy of it, then confirm the copy is correct. You need this for things like passports, birth certificates and driving licences.

Witness a written statement

If you need to write down something that is true and sign it officially, a JP can watch you sign it. Banks, courts and government offices often need this.

Give an oath

A JP can ask you to make a solemn promise that what you are saying is true. This is needed when you make a formal statement for court.

Sign a sworn statement

A JP can sign a written statement that you have sworn is true. Courts and government offices accept these statements when a JP has signed them.

Witness legal papers

A JP can watch you sign important legal papers, like permission forms or agreements, when you need an independent person to be there.

Help in your community

JPs connect people with the government. They can help if you have a worry about safety in your area or need to make an official complaint.

What a JP cannot do

A JP is not a lawyer and cannot give you legal advice. A JP cannot:

  • Give you legal advice or speak for you in court
  • Tell you if a document is legally correct
  • Check a copy without seeing the original document
  • Sign a document they have a personal stake in
  • Charge you money for their help
  • Stamp documents that are going to be used in another country (you need a notary public for that — see below)

Need a lawyer? You can find one through the Barbados Bar Association or by calling (246) 537-7316.

What is a notary public?

A notary public is not the same as a JP. A notary is a lawyer or chartered accountant who has been given extra powers by the Government. They can sign and stamp documents that need to be used outside Barbados.

You will need a notary, not a JP, if you are doing things like:

  • sending a document to a bank, school or office in another country
  • buying or selling land overseas
  • getting a document ready for an apostille from the Supreme Court (the special overseas stamp)

A notary will charge you a fee. Since September 2024, lawyers and chartered accountants with 15 or more years of experience can register as notaries. Your own lawyer or accountant may already be one — just ask them.

What to bring when you visit a JP

  • the original document and the copy you want checked — the JP needs to see both
  • a photo ID (passport, national ID card or driving licence)
  • any other documents the organisation asking you has requested

Do not sign your statement before you get there. You must sign it in front of the JP.

Tip: Phone the JP before you go. Some JPs work from home and may not be there during the day. Others prefer you visit them at their workplace.

How JPs are picked in Barbados

JPs are chosen by the Government of Barbados. The steps are:

  1. Members of Parliament suggest people from their area who would be good JPs.
  2. The Cabinet of Barbados looks at the names and decides who to pick.
  3. The President of Barbados officially appoints them.
  4. Each new JP takes an oath at a public ceremony before they can start work.

The list of JPs has grown a lot since 2023. More than 900 new JPs were added in 2024 and 2026 alone.

Need help finding a JP?

We do not show personal contact details for JPs online. To get in touch with one, call one of the offices below.

  • Office of the Attorney General Best place to start — they keep the official record.
    Phone: (246) 467-7370
    Email: agoffice@barbados.gov.bb
  • Barbados Judicial System For JP services linked to court matters.
    Phone: (246) 535-9700
    Website: barbadoslawcourts.gov.bb
  • Barbados Bar Association If you need a lawyer or a notary public.
    Phone: (246) 537-7316
    Website: barbadosbarassociation.com

Where this information comes from

The list of JPs on the next page is the official register kept by the Supreme Court of Barbados. The Supreme Court last updated the public list on 30 April 2023.

In October 2024 the Government appointed 344 new JPs, and in January 2026 a further 563 new JPs were sworn in at a public ceremony. Those names will be added to the public list when the Supreme Court next updates it.

Every JP appointment is officially announced in the Official Gazette of Barbados, published by the Government Printery. If you want to see the original notice for a JP, search the Gazette for the date of their swearing-in.

Sources: Supreme Court of Barbados — Register of Justices of the Peace (30 April 2023); the Official Gazette of Barbados (Government Printery) for the original appointment notices; Government of Barbados — public announcements of new JP appointments (October 2024, January 2026); Office of the Attorney General. Phone (246) 467-7370 to check whether someone is a JP.

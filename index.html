Justice of the Peace

A Justice of the Peace (JP) can help you with official documents. Find out what a JP does and how to find one near you.

Who is a Justice of the Peace?

A Justice of the Peace (JP) is someone appointed by the President of Barbados to help people with official documents.

JPs are ordinary people from your community — teachers, nurses, farmers, accountants, church leaders. When they are chosen, their parish and job are recorded so you can find them.

JPs do this job for free. They are not allowed to charge you. If you already know a JP — a neighbour, a teacher, a church elder, someone you work with — you can just go to them.

What can a JP help you with?

Most people visit a JP for one of three reasons. All three are free.

Certify a copy of a document
A JP looks at your original document and a copy of it, then signs the copy to confirm it matches. You need this for things like passport applications, school enrolments and overseas job applications.
Sworn statements and affidavits
If you need to put something in writing and swear it is true — a statutory declaration, an affidavit for a court matter, or a sworn statement for a bank or government office — a JP can watch you sign it, take your oath, and sign the document to confirm everything was done properly.
Witness a signature on legal papers
For documents that need an independent person to confirm you signed in their presence — like consent forms or simple agreements — a JP can be that witness.

What a JP cannot do

A JP is not a lawyer and cannot give you legal advice. A JP cannot:

  • Give you legal advice or speak for you in court
  • Tell you if a document is legally correct
  • Check a copy without seeing the original document
  • Sign a document they have a personal stake in
  • Charge you money for their help
  • Stamp documents that are going to be used in another country — for that you need a notary public, who is a lawyer or chartered accountant the Government has given extra powers to certify documents for overseas use

Need a lawyer? You can find one through the Barbados Bar Association or by calling (246) 537-7316.

What is a notary public?

A notary public is not the same as a JP. A notary is a lawyer or chartered accountant who has been given extra powers by the Government. They can sign and stamp documents that need to be used outside Barbados.

You will need a notary, not a JP, if you are doing things like:

  • sending a document to a bank, school or office in another country
  • buying or selling land overseas
  • getting a document ready for an apostille from the Supreme Court (the special overseas stamp)

A notary will charge you a fee. Since September 2024, lawyers and chartered accountants with 15 or more years of experience can register as notaries. Your own lawyer or accountant may already be one — just ask them.

What to bring when you visit a JP

  • the original document and the copy you want certified — the JP needs to see both
  • a photo ID (passport, national ID card or driving licence)
  • any other documents the organisation asking you has requested

If you are signing something in front of the JP — an affidavit, a sworn statement or a witnessed form — do not sign it before you arrive. You must sign in front of them.

Tip: Phone the JP before you go. Some JPs work from home and may not be there during the day. Others prefer you visit them at their workplace.

How JPs are picked in Barbados

JPs are appointed by the President of Barbados on the advice of the Cabinet. The steps are:

  1. The Cabinet of Barbados decides who to put forward as a JP.
  2. The President of Barbados officially appoints them.
  3. The appointment is published in the Official Gazette.
  4. Each new JP takes an oath at a public ceremony before they can start work.

Once someone becomes a JP, they stay a JP for life unless they choose to stop or are removed.

The list of JPs has grown a lot since 2023 — see where this information comes from for the exact numbers and dates.

Where this information comes from

The list of JPs on the next page is the official register kept by the Supreme Court of Barbados. The Supreme Court last updated the public list on 30 April 2023.

In 2024 the Government appointed 436 new JPs. You can download the official 2024 list (PDF, 15 pages) for the full names, addresses and parishes.

In January 2026 a further 563 new JPs were sworn in at a public ceremony. Those names will be added to the public list when the Supreme Court next updates it.

Every JP appointment is officially announced in the Official Gazette of Barbados, published by the Government Printery. If you want to see the original notice for a JP, search the Gazette for the date of their swearing-in.

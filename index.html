Justice of the Peace

Last updated on June 8th, 2026

A Justice of the Peace (JP) is appointed by the President of Barbados to help people with official documents: certifying copies, witnessing signatures, and taking sworn statements and affidavits. JPs are ordinary community members and their help is free of charge. They are not allowed to charge you.

How to find a Justice of the Peace

  1. Search the directory

    Look up JPs by parish or use your device's location. The directory lists every JP on the official register.

    Find a JP

  2. Get help by phone

    We do not show personal contact details for JPs online. If you cannot find one in the directory, contact one of these offices.

    Office of the Attorney General. Phone (246) 467-7370 or email agoffice@barbados.gov.bb.

    Barbados Judicial System, for JP services linked to court matters. Phone (246) 535-9700.

What to bring

  • The original document and the copy you want certified, so the JP can see both
  • A photo ID (passport, national ID card or driving licence)
  • Any other documents the organisation has asked for

If you need a document signed in front of the JP, do not sign it before you arrive.

What a JP can do

  • Certify a copy of a document, like a passport, birth certificate or driving licence
  • Witness sworn statements and affidavits
  • Witness your signature on legal papers, like consent forms

What a JP cannot do

  • Give legal advice or speak for you in court
  • Certify a copy without seeing the original document
  • Sign a document they have a personal interest in
  • Charge you a fee
  • Stamp documents for use in another country (you'll need a notary public)

If you need a notary public

A notary public is a lawyer or chartered accountant with additional powers granted by the Government to certify documents for use outside Barbados. A JP cannot do this work. You need a notary.

For the official process, fees, and where to go, see Get a document notarised.

If you live overseas

If you are outside Barbados and a document needs to be certified, you can usually use a notary public or commissioner of oaths in the country where you are. The certified document will be accepted in most cases.

For matters that specifically require a Barbados JP, contact the nearest Barbados High Commission or Consulate, or call the Office of the Attorney General on +1 246 467-7370 for advice.

Where this information comes from

The directory uses the Supreme Court of Barbados register (last updated 30 April 2023), the official 2024 list (PDF) and the JPs appointed in January 2026. Every appointment is gazetted in the Official Gazette of Barbados. To check whether someone is currently a JP, phone the Office of the Attorney General on (246) 467-7370.
